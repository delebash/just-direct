- **A fake must answer in the real thing's shape.** The routes read `r.content` from `services.run`, the fakes
  answered `{content}`, and the kit's `runAction` answers `{text}`: every real plan and frame check failed while every
  test passed. `services.run` now maps the kit's reply in one place, held by `server/tests/services.test.js` against
  the kit's own `LLMResponse`. Before calling a fault "the model", read the raw reply the app received.
- Two fixes built on the wrong cause were removed the same hour: a retry of an "empty" planner answer and the
  "high" thinking level (the real run's seven visible tries all answered).
- Vitest lets a named import its module doesn't export pass as undefined; `server/tests/boot.test.js` loads the
  server under Node.

- **A rule is quoted only inside its scope.** "Neither app calls or spawns the other at runtime" is the JustWrite ↔
  JustVoice boundary (JustVoice `docs/dev/design-decisions.md` §3, its reason: JustVoice stands alone). It was cited as
  a family rule against JustDirect calling JustVoice — to back a design already started. Read a rule's heading and
  its reason before citing it; the family rules expect "other apps calling the API".
- **A copy of another app's call reproduces its shape, not its tested recipe.** JustDirect sent JustVoice's request
  (`instructions` on Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign, JustVoice `slot.js:683`) but designed every line afresh from the card's
  words; JustVoice designs once from a model-written description and clones each line from that audition
  (`book_voices/index.js`). The result: a voice the user heard as male for a woman.
- **No release is needed to run an engine** — the family runs development builds (above); never say a step waits for
  a release.
- **Decide, attack, write down, then build.** The line routes were half removed for a design not yet thought through.
- A PowerShell hashtable replacement once corrupted two files; edit with the Edit tool.
- `git commit -F -` with a PowerShell here-string fails; write the message to a file in the scratchpad.
- A test film's sides are 384–1920 in steps of 64; the kit's UiTag has no `warning` intent; UiNumber's widths are

## The voice decision (waits for the user — it changes their round E decision)

The user's question, 2026-11-07: "do we use jv or just take what we need and put it in just direct". Shown to the user
2026-11-07 as Claude's rec; nothing built until they answer.

**What a film's voices need:** (1) a woman sounds like a woman, of her age; (2) one character, one voice, in every
line; (3) the voice exists before its talking clip (LTX-2.3 draws the mouth to it, PRD); (4) the default models allow
commercial use (PRD: "Every model in the default route allows commercial use"); (5) it fits one 8 GB card beside
LTX-2.3, FLUX.2 and llama.cpp; (6) a line the planner or the user changes is spoken again in a click.

**The options:** A — hand the lines to JustVoice as a file (its standard JSON in, `export_voicelines` WAVs out) and
bring them back; B — JustDirect drives JustVoice's server over HTTP; C — the voice-making part of JustVoice moves into
the kit, one copy both apps run; D — copy what JustDirect needs out of JustVoice ("take what we need").

**Pass 1, the sourced rules.** D breaks "extract shared code instead of adding another copy" (the global rules) and
"Copies drift and share bugs" (the family rules) — and D is what failed: JustDirect's own Qwen3 rows and hand-built

## Decisions

1. **One film per project, its work in tabs: Story · Cards · Shots · Cut.** Attack: Nomi's node canvas does more, but
   the record's plan is stages (cards, shots, clips, assembly) and a canvas is a second screen model in a family that
   has none; tabs follow the family's one-scroller pages. Survives.
2. **The film's files live in `data/films/<filmId>/` and go in the backup.** Attack: clips make backups large — but
   they are the user's work, and an MP4 clip is 5–20 MB; the export folder is left out (it can be made again).
3. **The planner writes cards and shots in two passes, then revises later shots after a hand edit** — the planner
   test's design and prompts, which passed. Attack: three passes take about 6 minutes a plan with thinking on; the
   user accepted minutes per step for quality.
4. **First frames: 3 candidates a shot by default (a setting), checked by the vision model, the best kept first.**
   Attack: 3 × 20–45 s plus the check is slow — but the handoff shows klein adds stray objects on most seeds, so one
   frame often fails; the count is a setting.
5. **Clips: one pass at 1280×704 is the default** (the user's third review: "one pass is the default"); a long take
   (up to 13 s) uses the two-stage recipe; a talking shot is one pass with its voice line; paths and guide videos add
   their LoRA. The clip's length comes from the shot's seconds (4n+1 frames at 24 fps).
6. **Voices: a character card carries a voice description; the line is spoken by audio.cpp's voice design model.**
   Attack: a cloned voice is better for a real actor, but needs the user's recording; a designed voice needs nothing.
   Cloning from a recording is added once design works.
7. **The cut: shots in plan order, each with its chosen clip, trims and a gap-free join; music and effects as tracks
   at a start time; export to MP4.** Attack: no transitions beyond a cut, a dissolve and a fade through black (the
   three the planner writes), no colour — the PRD puts a full editing suite out of scope.
