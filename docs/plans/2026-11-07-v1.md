## Picks Claude made under the go (not the user's text), each attacked

   *Carry it forward* off that shot, so a cut tweak stops a later revision fixing its action. Kept: the planner writes
   transitions, and a revision would overwrite a hand-set one otherwise; the help says so.
2. **A line whose words changed after it was spoken shows "Words changed"; its voice is kept.** Attack: the export
   still uses the old words — but the clip was made with that voice and its mouth matches it; deleting it would lose
   work without asking. The tag says to speak it again and remake the clip.
3. **A talking clip is held to 13 s** (LTX's longest) however long its line — a fault found while reading
   (`clips_api.js` asked for a clip as long as any line).
4. **The export is refused before its job without FFmpeg**, naming the AI page's FFmpeg tab (the kit's
   `ffmpegReadiness`), instead of failing inside the job.
5. **Voices are a setting, not a routed feature** (`voices.model` through `withSpeechModel`, as JustVoice speaks).
   Attack: no install banner for voices — the refusal's words name the Speech tab instead; the kit's banner takes
   only routed features.
6. **The music and effects form names its model and licence** from the feature's readiness (the model row's
   `license`) — the PRD's "each with its licence label".
8. **First frames paint the missing pictures of the cards they take as references** (looks excluded, a card with a
   picture never repainted). Attack: it paints pictures nobody asked for and adds about 30 s a card — but a frame
   painted without its cards' pictures loses the faces and places, the app's core promise, and the user asked for

7. **The Cut tab's timeline is drawn by the app** (the standards table's "app" row). Attack one, the family rule
   against hand-rolling: neither Quasar nor the kit ships a timeline, and it only shows lengths — editing is the kit's
   number boxes, selects and sliders. Attack two, a timeline package (vis-timeline and the like): those are date-event
   or full editor timelines, a second interaction model for a display the PRD keeps simple. Nothing new on a third

  test passed. `services.run` now maps the kit's reply in one place, held by `server/tests/services.test.js` against
  the kit's own `LLMResponse`. Before calling a fault "the model", read the raw reply the app received.

  "high" thinking level (the real run's seven visible tries all answered).

## The kit changes it needs (filled in the kit first)

1. **Thinking beside a JSON schema.** The kit forces thinking off whenever an action is in JSON mode (`llm/prompts.js`
   `_effectiveThink`, its B3 guardrail). The planner test proved the opposite for a strict schema on llama-server:
   revisions were correct on 3 of 3 plans only with thinking on (feasibility §J.3). Change: an action whose contract
   is a strict `json_schema` may think when its preset says so; `json_object` stays forced off. Attack: other apps'
   actions with a schema and a thinking preset would change behaviour — checked by grep in the change (their presets'
   `think` on schema actions), and their suites run after it.
2. **The sd.cpp build.** The kit's pinned sd.cpp must be our `jd` release (the LTX guide, audio-to-video, the 8 GB
   memory fixes): the kit's TASKS, "JustDirect's six decisions", item 1 (in progress: release run 42431109657).

## Decisions

1. **One film per project, its work in tabs: Story · Cards · Shots · Cut.** Attack: Nomi's node canvas does more, but
   the record's plan is stages (cards, shots, clips, assembly) and a canvas is a second screen model in a family that
   has none; tabs follow the family's one-scroller pages. Survives.
2. **The film's files live in `data/films/<filmId>/` and go in the backup.** Attack: clips make backups large — but
   they are the user's work, and an MP4 clip is 5–20 MB; the export folder is left out (it can be made again).
3. **The planner writes cards and shots in two passes, then revises later shots after a hand edit** — the planner
   test's design and prompts, which passed. Attack: three passes take about 6 minutes a plan with thinking on; the
   user accepted minutes per step for quality.
4. **First frames: 3 candidates a shot by default (a setting), checked by the vision model, the best kept first.**
   Attack: 3 × 20–45 s plus the check is slow — but the handoff shows klein adds stray objects on most seeds, so one
   frame often fails; the count is a setting.
5. **Clips: one pass at 1280×704 is the default** (the user's third review: "one pass is the default"); a long take
   (up to 13 s) uses the two-stage recipe; a talking shot is one pass with its voice line; paths and guide videos add
   their LoRA. The clip's length comes from the shot's seconds (4n+1 frames at 24 fps).
6. **Voices: a character card carries a voice description; the line is spoken by audio.cpp's voice design model.**
   Attack: a cloned voice is better for a real actor, but needs the user's recording; a designed voice needs nothing.
   Cloning from a recording is added once design works.
7. **The cut: shots in plan order, each with its chosen clip, trims and a gap-free join; music and effects as tracks
   at a start time; export to MP4.** Attack: no transitions beyond a cut, a dissolve and a fade through black (the
   three the planner writes), no colour — the PRD puts a full editing suite out of scope.
