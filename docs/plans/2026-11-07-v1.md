## Picks Claude made under the go (not the user's text), each attacked

1. **A transition changed on the Cut tab is a hand edit** — the shot is marked *Changed by you*. Attack: it keeps
   *Carry it forward* off that shot, so a cut tweak stops a later revision fixing its action. Kept: the planner writes
   transitions, and a revision would overwrite a hand-set one otherwise; the help says so.
2. **A line whose words changed after it was spoken shows "Words changed"; its voice is kept.** Attack: the export
   still uses the old words — but the clip was made with that voice and its mouth matches it; deleting it would lose
   work without asking. The tag says to speak it again and remake the clip.
3. **A talking clip is held to 13 s** (LTX's longest) however long its line — a fault found while reading
   (`clips_api.js` asked for a clip as long as any line).
4. **The export is refused before its job without FFmpeg**, naming the AI page's FFmpeg tab (the kit's
   `ffmpegReadiness`), instead of failing inside the job.
5. *(Under review — "The voice decision" below.)* **Voices are a setting, not a routed feature** (`voices.model`
   through `withSpeechModel`, as JustVoice speaks).
   Attack: no install banner for voices — the refusal's words name the Speech tab instead; the kit's banner takes
   only routed features.
6. **The music and effects form names its model and licence** from the feature's readiness (the model row's
   `license`) — the PRD's "each with its licence label".
9. **Songs are music with lyrics** (ACE-Step 1.5 Turbo through its own feature, `sounds.song`), not a third kind of
   sound. Attack: a separate kind would sort and label more plainly — but a song plays, moves and mixes exactly as
   music does, and a kind needs a table rebuild in SQLite (a CHECK constraint); the list shows "Song" from its lyrics.
10. *(Under review — "The voice decision" below.)* **A voice from a recording** (decision 6's "cloning once design
   works"): Qwen3-TTS Base 1.7B, JustVoice's row and
   request (the clip as `voice_ref`, its words as `reference_text`, else `x_vector_only_mode`). Attack: cloning a
   real person's voice — the help asks for a voice the user has the right to use, and nothing leaves this computer.
   Recorded in the window through the kit's `recordMicrophone`; any file FFmpeg reads.
8. **First frames paint the missing pictures of the cards they take as references** (looks excluded, a card with a

## Mistakes not to repeat

- **A fake must answer in the real thing's shape.** The routes read `r.content` from `services.run`, the fakes
  answered `{content}`, and the kit's `runAction` answers `{text}`: every real plan and frame check failed while every
  test passed. `services.run` now maps the kit's reply in one place, held by `server/tests/services.test.js` against
  the kit's own `LLMResponse`. Before calling a fault "the model", read the raw reply the app received.
- Two fixes built on the wrong cause were removed the same hour: a retry of an "empty" planner answer and the
  "high" thinking level (the real run's seven visible tries all answered).
- Vitest lets a named import its module doesn't export pass as undefined; `server/tests/boot.test.js` loads the
  server under Node.

- **A rule is quoted only inside its scope.** "Neither app calls or spawns the other at runtime" is the JustWrite ↔
  JustVoice boundary (JustVoice `docs/dev/design-decisions.md` §3, its reason: JustVoice stands alone). It was cited as
  a family rule against JustDirect calling JustVoice — to back a design already started. Read a rule's heading and
  its reason before citing it; the family rules expect "other apps calling the API".
- **A copy of another app's call reproduces its shape, not its tested recipe.** JustDirect sent JustVoice's request
  (`instructions` on Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign, JustVoice `slot.js:683`) but designed every line afresh from the card's
  words; JustVoice designs once from a model-written description and clones each line from that audition
  (`book_voices/index.js`). The result: a voice the user heard as male for a woman.
- **No release is needed to run an engine** — the family runs development builds (above); never say a step waits for
  a release.
- **Decide, attack, write down, then build.** The line routes were half removed for a design not yet thought through.
- A PowerShell hashtable replacement once corrupted two files; edit with the Edit tool.
- `git commit -F -` with a PowerShell here-string fails; write the message to a file in the scratchpad.
- A test film's sides are 384–1920 in steps of 64; the kit's UiTag has no `warning` intent; UiNumber's widths are
  `num`, `token`, `id`, `name`, …; a QSelect opens in a test with `vm.showPopup()`.

## The voice decision (waits for the user — it changes their round E decision)

The user's question, 2026-11-07: "do we use jv or just take what we need and put it in just direct". Shown to the user
2026-11-07 as Claude's rec; nothing built until they answer.

**What a film's voices need:** (1) a woman sounds like a woman, of her age; (2) one character, one voice, in every
line; (3) the voice exists before its talking clip (LTX-2.3 draws the mouth to it, PRD); (4) the default models allow
commercial use (PRD: "Every model in the default route allows commercial use"); (5) it fits one 8 GB card beside
LTX-2.3, FLUX.2 and llama.cpp; (6) a line the planner or the user changes is spoken again in a click.

**The options:** A — hand the lines to JustVoice as a file (its standard JSON in, `export_voicelines` WAVs out) and
bring them back; B — JustDirect drives JustVoice's server over HTTP; C — the voice-making part of JustVoice moves into
the kit, one copy both apps run; D — copy what JustDirect needs out of JustVoice ("take what we need").

**Pass 1, the sourced rules.** D breaks "extract shared code instead of adding another copy" (the global rules) and
"Copies drift and share bugs" (the family rules) — and D is what failed: JustDirect's own Qwen3 rows and hand-built
requests (`seed_presets.js:407, 430`, `sounds_api.js`) matched JustVoice's request and missed its recipe. C is what
the rules ask for ("Anything that can be the same across apps is one implementation in the kit", E:\Dev\Web\CLAUDE.md;
"Every AI engine and model goes through the kit … its models catalog rows", the family rules) — but round E kept
JustVoice's models and its per-family requests in JustVoice (JustVoice `docs/plans/2026-10-29-round-e-onto-the-kit.md`
§1, decided on the user's go), so C changes the user's decision. No rule rules out A or B (the JustWrite ↔ JustVoice
file rule covers those two apps only).

**Pass 2, the needs.** A puts an app switch in the middle of the film (before the talking clips, need 3) and again for
every changed line (need 6). B runs two speech runtimes in two processes on one 8 GB card; JustVoice's own record has
two copies of a model filling this card (its AGENTS.md, 2026-10-04), and "our apps don't manage each other's memory"
(the global rules) — JustDirect could not make JustVoice let go before a clip; JustDirect would not work without
JustVoice running; JustVoice's API is shaped for its own screens (its first endpoint table went stale). C meets 1–3, 5
and 6 in one process under one memory arbiter.

**Pass 3, what is actually tested.** JustVoice's tested pair is Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign (`qwen3-vd`, commercial "Yes")
designing and Breeze TTS 2 (`breeze_tts`) cloning (JustVoice `models.js:517-519`). Breeze's licence: "Any Commercial
Purpose involving … an Output requires a separate written commercial license from BreezeBlue" (JustVoice
`curated/breeze_tts.js:22-23`, RESEARCH §10.13) — so no option gives JustDirect the tested pair as its default (need
4). JustVoice's round 4 (its RESEARCH §10.22) scored nine cloning setups; the Apache-2.0 ones were CosyVoice3 and
VoxCPM2; Qwen3-TTS Base (JustDirect's clone row) was not among them. What carries over as tested: the description's
form (gender and age first, then pitch, timbre, pace, manner), designing once and cloning every line from that
audition, and each model family's request. The cloning model needs a listening test on this card whichever option is
chosen. The cause of the male voice is not proven: the request field matched JustVoice's; the words (the card's "an
older woman's low, weathered voice") and the per-line redesign differed.

**Pass 4, cost and surprise.** C costs most: a kit change (frozen now), JustVoice moved onto it in a JustVoice session
(after the family release) with its own tests, then JustDirect built on it. It answers "neither" to the user's either/or
— but it is the family's own pattern: music, effects and lip-sync are already kit audio features (`runAudioFeature`);
speech is the one engine whose models live in an app, and only because JustVoice was the only app that spoke on
2026-10-29. That premise is now false.

**Pass 5, if the user says no to C.** A, not B: A's cost is the user's time; B's is a card that runs out of memory.

**Rec:** C, narrow — the kit gets JustVoice's speech model rows and notes, its per-family requests (`slot.js`) and

## The kit changes it needs (filled in the kit first)

1. **Thinking beside a JSON schema.** The kit forces thinking off whenever an action is in JSON mode (`llm/prompts.js`
   `_effectiveThink`, its B3 guardrail). The planner test proved the opposite for a strict schema on llama-server:
   revisions were correct on 3 of 3 plans only with thinking on (feasibility §J.3). Change: an action whose contract
   is a strict `json_schema` may think when its preset says so; `json_object` stays forced off. Attack: other apps'
   actions with a schema and a thinking preset would change behaviour — checked by grep in the change (their presets'
   `think` on schema actions), and their suites run after it.
2. **The sd.cpp build.** The kit's pinned sd.cpp must be our `jd` release (the LTX guide, audio-to-video, the 8 GB
   memory fixes): the kit's TASKS, "JustDirect's six decisions", item 1. Release run 42431109657 is green; the pin
   commit waits for "freeze lifted". Until then the app runs the `jd` development build (`dev_builds.js`).

## Decisions

1. **One film per project, its work in tabs: Story · Cards · Shots · Cut.** Attack: Nomi's node canvas does more, but
   the record's plan is stages (cards, shots, clips, assembly) and a canvas is a second screen model in a family that
   has none; tabs follow the family's one-scroller pages. Survives.
2. **The film's files live in `data/films/<filmId>/` and go in the backup.** Attack: clips make backups large — but
   they are the user's work, and an MP4 clip is 5–20 MB; the export folder is left out (it can be made again).
3. **The planner writes cards and shots in two passes, then revises later shots after a hand edit** — the planner
   test's design and prompts, which passed. Attack: three passes take about 6 minutes a plan with thinking on; the
   user accepted minutes per step for quality.
4. **First frames: 3 candidates a shot by default (a setting), checked by the vision model, the best kept first.**
   Attack: 3 × 20–45 s plus the check is slow — but the handoff shows klein adds stray objects on most seeds, so one
   frame often fails; the count is a setting.
5. **Clips: one pass at 1280×704 is the default** (the user's third review: "one pass is the default"); a long take
   (up to 13 s) uses the two-stage recipe; a talking shot is one pass with its voice line; paths and guide videos add
   their LoRA. The clip's length comes from the shot's seconds (4n+1 frames at 24 fps).
6. **Voices: a character card carries a voice description; the line is spoken by audio.cpp's voice design model.**
   Attack: a cloned voice is better for a real actor, but needs the user's recording; a designed voice needs nothing.
   Cloning from a recording is added once design works.
7. **The cut: shots in plan order, each with its chosen clip, trims and a gap-free join; music and effects as tracks
   at a start time; export to MP4.** Attack: no transitions beyond a cut, a dissolve and a fade through black (the
   three the planner writes), no colour — the PRD puts a full editing suite out of scope.
